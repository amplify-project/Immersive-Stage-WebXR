Sources (files)

Checks the declared channel order against the recording, and measures how far the ambisonic mic was turned from the camera. Needs the stems placed below: it compares where each musician sounds with where you say they are.

Build a bed from separate W/X/Y/Z files

Pick the mono file for each ambisonic component. They are written in the order the Format above expects — FuMa as W,X,Y,Z and AmbiX as W,Y,Z,X — so label them by component, not by the position they had in the recorder. Channel order is not something Analyse bed can fix afterwards: get it right here.

Gains are left alone: FuMa components stay FuMa. Existing files are never overwritten.

Live sources

Video comes in on its own source; all audio (FOA + stems) arrives together on a single multichannel device/stream (e.g. the X32 over USB). Each musician is assigned to a channel below.

bridge stopped

Creates the virtual camera /dev/video10 (Insta360 Virtual) and feeds it via the SDK. After initializing, select it above as the USB source.

If the Insta360 stitching delays the video, the audio arrives early. Raise the delay (ms) until they line up; a negative value advances the audio.

monitor stopped

📐 Auto-measure delay: measures the offset automatically — it captures ~8 s while you give a sharp clap in front of the camera (a single action: the hit is heard and seen at once; a clapperboard works too), and fills the field with the ms (as an absolute value: the video always arrives late). No DISPLAY needed. Equivalent to ./measure_sync.sh. 📷 ROI: narrows the region of the frame where you'll clap to sharpen detection. ⚠ it uses the ALSA audio exclusively → stop it before going live.

no ROI · detected across the whole frame

Check this only if the 4 capsules arrive raw (A-format). If the FOA is already converted to B-format upstream (plugin/DAW), leave it unchecked.

Musicians / stems

Drag the dots on the radar to place each musician. The order here = channel order in the stream.

3D placement · 360 sphere (top-down)

Up = front of the video (0°). Left = +90°. Right = −90°. What a musician looks like is not here: the 3D model is only seen in passthrough, so it lives with the rest of AR in AR room below.

Height · 360 sphere (front view)

Azimuth across, height up — the band unrolled in front of you. Drag a dot up or down to raise or lower that musician; its azimuth stays where the radar left it. In the 360 sphere a musician is a direction, not a point: these metres are that angle read at 1.6 m, only so you have something to compare against the room. For AR, place the musician in the room below.

AR room · passthrough (metres)

Where each musician really stands in the room: a point in metres, not a direction. AR is not the 360 sphere — the listener walks among the sources, so distance and height are heard. Top view first (x across, front of the room up), then the same room from the front (x across, height up).

A musician with no room position is drawn hollow, at the spot the player falls back to (the 360 direction at 1.6 m). Dragging it — or Place all — fixes it there, changing nothing you can hear, and from then on it is a real point you can move. Unplace hands it back to the sphere. The origin is where the room alignment puts you: the same reference the headset calibrates against, not any one spectator.

3D model (AR)

Mesh height is how tall the figure is, feet to head — not where it is heard. That is Height m above (or Elevation on the sphere): the model hangs from the point that sounds down to the floor, so raising one never moves the other.

Preview · placed the way the headset will

Exporters that work Z-up (Blender, CAD) hand the figure over rotated −90° about X. Bad orientation is nearly always axis-shaped, which is why this is a tick box and not an angle.

Drag sideways to turn the model (writes Yaw); drag up and down to look from higher or lower; wheel to get closer. The grey figure is 1.70 m, the camera is where the audience will be, and the amber arrow is where yaw points — not a guess at which way the model actually faces.

Uploads land in meshes/ on this machine and are offered to every musician. .glb only — a .gltf drags its textures and buffers along as separate files, which one upload cannot carry.
